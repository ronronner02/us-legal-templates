CERTIFICATE – GUARDIANSHIP CAPACITY ASSESSMENT 
(Md. Rule 10-202) 
NOTE: This certificate will be used in a legal proceeding to determine whether an individual needs a 
guardian of person, a guardian of the property, or both. It can also be used to help identify less restrictive 
alternatives (options) that can help limit or modify a guardianship. Only a licensed: physician, 
psychologist, physician assistant, clinical social worker – clinical (LCSW-C), or nurse practitioner 
may complete this certificate. 
TO THE CLINICIAN COMPLETING THIS DOCUMENT: 
Guardianship is a court process in which a court determines that an individual needs another person – a 
guardian – to make some or all decisions on the individual’s behalf. Guardians of the person are 
appointed to make personal decisions on behalf of an individual under guardianship, including decisions 
about medical care, personal needs, housing, educational needs, and access to family and friends. 
Guardians of the property are appointed to make financial decisions, manage property, or apply for 
benefits on behalf of the individual under guardianship. 
Guardianship restricts the fundamental rights and liberties of the individual and is intended to be a 
last resort. When a guardian is appointed, the individual will no longer be able to make their own 
decisions, unless the court limits the guardian’s authority. 
Courts will consider alternatives to guardianship, which are formal or informal options that, alone or 
together, allow an individual to have their needs met without a guardian. Alternatives to guardianship can 
also help the court limit a guardian’s authority, allowing the individual to retain the right to make some 
decisions. 
Your assessment will help the court determine the individual’s functional capacity to make and 
communicate responsible decisions about his/her/their personal or financial affairs. The information you 
provide can also help identify alternatives to guardianship or limit the authority of any appointed guardian. 
For more information about guardianship and its alternatives, visit www.mdcourts.gov/idealguide. 
This certificate must be based on your personal examination or evaluation of the individual. You must 
complete and sign it yourself. Complete the entire form. If your assessment does not allow you to 
provide an opinion or to complete a particular section, explain or write “Not applicable” or “N/A”. 
Attach additional documents if needed. The court may reject your certificate if a section is left blank. 
You may be asked to testify about your assessment at a hearing.
