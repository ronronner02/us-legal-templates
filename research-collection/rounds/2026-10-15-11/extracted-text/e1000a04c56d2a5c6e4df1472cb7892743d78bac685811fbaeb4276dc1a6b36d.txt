Instructions on How to File a Small Claims Lawsuit 
 
Use this packet to file a court case for money if the amount is $5,000 or less, not 
including court costs (fees for using the court). When you are done, file the case with 
the Clerk of the District Court – Small Claims Division. 
 
Need Other Services? See page 7 of this packet. 
 
This Small Claims Lawsuit form has five (5) parts: 
  
 Part 1: Statement and Reason for Claim  
 Part 2: Military Service Affidavit  
 Part 3: Expert Witness  
 Part 4: No Appeal  
 Part 5: Signature and Notary 
 
Part 1 – Statement and Reason for Claim 
  
Plaintiff: The plaintiff is the person who files a court case for money. You are the 
plaintiff in this case. 
 
Defendant: The defendant is the person or company who owes you money.  
 
There may be more than one plaintiff or defendant in a case. If you have more than 
one plaintiff or defendant, see page 6 to add the information.  
 
Use this section to tell the court about yourself and the defendant. Tell the court how 
much the defendant owes you and why.

Part 2 – Military Service Affidavit 
Federal law protects servicemembers who are defendants in a lawsuit while they are 
deployed or on active duty. This protects the servicemember from a court judgment 
(decision of the lawsuit) entered against them without first receiving notice of the 
lawsuit and a chance to defend the case. Find the law: 50 U.S.C.A. § 3931 and G.L. 
1956 § 30-7-10.

Part 4 – No Appeal  
 
I understand that I must give up my right to an appeal. An appeal is when another 
court reviews your  case. By law, you must agree that even if you do not like the 
outcome of the case, you cannot challenge the decision made by the judge. Find the 
law: G.L. 1956 § 10-16-4(b).

Step 6: If the defendant does not answer the lawsuit, the court can enter

the money you are owed without the defendant being there.   
Step 7: If the defenda nt does answer the lawsuit, the court will refer th e case 
to mediation. You must contact the mediator by the date in the notice that the court 
will send to you.   
Step 8: Prepare for trial. If the mediation was not successful, the court will 
send you a hearing notice with a trial date. Gather evidence, witnesses, and plan 
what you are going to say.
