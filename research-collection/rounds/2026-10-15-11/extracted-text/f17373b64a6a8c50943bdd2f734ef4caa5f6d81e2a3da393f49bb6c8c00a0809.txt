Answer to Small Claims Lawsuit

You have been sued! Follow the directions below. If you do not, the 
court can give the person who sued you everything they asked for in 
this lawsuit against you. 
 
Instructions 
 
Use this packet to file an answer to a Small Claims Lawsuit. A Small Claims Lawsuit 
is a court case for money if the amount is $5,000 or less, not including court costs 
(fees for using the court).  When you are done, file this form  with the Clerk of the 
District Court – Small Claims Division. 
 
Need Other Services? See page 7 of this packet. 
 
 
The Answer to Small Claims Lawsuit has seven (7) parts: 
 Part 1: Plaintiff’s Statement of Claim 
 Part 2: Answer  
 Part 3: Expert Witness 
 Part 4: Counterclaim  
 Part 5: No Appeal of the Counterclaim  
 Part 6: Signature  
 Part 7: Certificate of Service 
 
 
Plaintiff: The plaintiff is the person who files a court case for money.  
 
Defendant: The defendant is the person or company who may owe the plaintiff 
money. You are the defendant in this case. 
 
There may be more than one plaintiff or defendant in a case.

Step 1: Read Part 1 - Statement of Claim by the plaintiff carefully. This is where 
the plaintiff tells the court why you owe money to the plaintiff and how much. See 
page 3 of this packet. 
 
Step 2: Fill out the Answer form. An answer is a written response to the Small 
Claims Lawsuit. The Answer form begins on page 3 of this packet. 
 
Step 3: File your Answer within twenty (20) days of service. Service  means 
delivery of court papers to you personally by a sheriff or constable. Service is how 
you find out about the lawsuit.  
 
Step 4: If you do not file an Answer , the court can enter a judgment by default. 
This means the court can order you to pay the money the plaintiff claims that you 
owe without you being there.

Step 5: The court will refer the case to mediation when you timely file an Answer 
(see Step 3 on page 2). You must contact the mediator by the date in the notice that 
the court will send to you.   
 
Step 5: Prepare for trial . If the mediation was not successful, the court will send 
you a hearing notice with a trial date. Gather evidence, witnesses, and plan what you 
are going to say. 
 
Step 6: Go to the trial. If you do not go to the trial, the court can enter a judgment 
by default. This means the court can order you to pay the money the plaintiff claims 
that you owe without you being there.
