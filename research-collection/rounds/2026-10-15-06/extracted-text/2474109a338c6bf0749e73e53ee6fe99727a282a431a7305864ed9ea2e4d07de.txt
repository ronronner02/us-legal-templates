2. Marital Property
NOTE: Marital property includes all assets acquired by you or your spouse from the date of
marriage until the date of divorce. It does not matter how the property is titled or who paid for it.
Property can be entirely marital, entirely non-marital, or partly marital and non-marital.
• Marital property can include real estate, vehicles, business interests, bank accounts, and
contributions to a retirement account made during your marriage.  It can also include equity
in a home that results from reducing the mortgage balance during the marriage.
• Marital property does not include property acquired by one party by inheritance or gift
from a third party, property acquired before the marriage, property excluded by a valid
agreement, or property directly traceable to any of these sources.

4. Child Support
NOTE: Child support is an obligation owed to your minor child(ren) and cannot be waived by the
parents. The court has the authority to establish child support, regardless of whether the parties have 
asked for a child support determination and regardless of any agreement the parties have reached. 
The court may accept an agreement on child support if: (1) the agreement is consistent with the 
Child Support Guidelines Worksheet; or (2) the court determines that any deviation from the 
Guidelines as agreed-upon by the parties is in the best interests of the child(ren).
